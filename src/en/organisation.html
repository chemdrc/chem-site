Organisation

A medical association, born in Bukavu

CHEM adheres to international humanitarian law. Its work rests on humanity, impartiality, neutrality, equality and independence.

Overview

Congo Health Emergency (CHEM) is a non-governmental, non-profit medical humanitarian organisation. It was founded in Bukavu, the capital of South Kivu, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, under the legal framework for non-profit associations.

The head office is in Bukavu. The association is founded for an indefinite period. The statutes provide for action across the whole country, and for the possibility of acting abroad if the competent bodies so decide. Initially, CHEM focuses on South Kivu. Branch offices may be opened where needs justify it.

History

CHEM was founded in Bukavu on 31 December 2021 by a founding general assembly. It was born where vulnerable and marginalised people, or those affected by conflict, disasters and other emergencies, struggle to access care and protection.

The organisation was designed for chronic crises, emergencies, post-emergency settings and development, as well as rehabilitation and construction. Its members come in particular from the medical, obstetric, nursing, administrative, social and agro-pastoral fields: a deliberately multidisciplinary team.

Vision

CHEM aspires to an inclusive world where all vulnerable and marginalised people have access to quality basic medical care, in modern health facilities, in line with World Health Organization standards.

Mission

The mission comes down to three complementary actions.

  • Care Give people real access to health, with a holistic approach to the person, and encourage their participation in the work.
  • Protect Help shield people from inequality and exclusion, ill-treatment and the dangers linked to human life and the environment.
  • Bear witness Document the realities experienced on the ground and challenge local, regional, national and international authorities on what affects the health and rights of vulnerable and marginalised people.

Principles and values

CHEM bases its work on five humanitarian principles.

  • Humanity
  • Impartiality
  • Neutrality
  • Equality
  • Independence
  • Health Putting health at the centre: a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease.
  • Resilience Supporting people so they can get through what they have experienced and thrive despite the risks.
  • Empowerment Enabling people in vulnerable situations to defend their rights and strengthen their social support.
  • Commitment Acting with determination to care, protect and bear witness, and to support communities through inclusive change.

Governance

The statutes establish four bodies. The meeting frequencies shown are those set out in the texts.

  1. General assembly

    Supreme body. It approves activity reports, the budget, the financial statements and the main strategic directions. Meets annually, with extraordinary meetings when necessary.

  2. Board of directors

    Decision-making body. It ensures sound organisation, oversees management, prepares action programmes and represents the association. It decides on commitments made with external partners. Meets quarterly.

  3. Audit committee

    It is responsible for auditing the association's accounts.

  4. Executive management

    Day-to-day management body. It coordinates and implements activities, defines key strategies, produces the annual activity report and maintains relations with the governing bodies and partners. Meets monthly.

Executive management includes an executive director (country director), a coordinator, a technical director, a finance officer and an administration and human resources officer. Depending on needs, growth and resources, this structure may be expanded.

Emblem

The motto is “Health for those in need” (in French, « Santé pour les nécessiteux »). The emblem brings together four colours — red, blue, bright green and white. The statutes associate them with urgency, kindness, trust, safety, healing, cleanliness and peace.
