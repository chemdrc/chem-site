Our work

Care, protect, bear witness

This page describes the scope set by the statutes. CHEM is a young organisation: the current phase focuses on South Kivu.

On this page Objectives Areas How we work People Areas of operation Priorities

Objectives

The overall objective is to contribute to integral development by promoting the health of suffering populations and universal health coverage, in order to reduce morbidity, mortality and social inequality.

Five specific objectives follow from it.

  1. Protect the environment and fight diseases linked to its degradation and to poor water, hygiene and sanitation.
  2. Promote social inclusion and gender equality, defend women's rights, encourage community engagement, and assist victims of conflict and other disasters.
  3. Promote integrated health and maternal and child health, and fight infectious diseases, malnutrition and abuse.
  4. Assist survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, orphans, widows and widowers, children formerly associated with armed groups, displaced people and refugees, people with reduced mobility and other vulnerable people.
  5. Create and organise medical, surgical and pharmaceutical activities and facilities, to meet needs and to prevent illness or assist those affected by it.

Areas of intervention

Where the statutes do not detail how the work is done, only the area is named.

01 Health

Primary health care, sexual and reproductive health, epidemic, endemic or pandemic emergencies, surgical emergencies, mental health, and health information, education and communication.

02 Protection

Child protection, the fight against sexual and gender-based violence, and the fight against torture and other human rights violations.

03 Gender and social inclusion

This area ties in with the objective of inclusion, defending women's rights and community engagement.

04 Environment and WASH

Water, hygiene and sanitation, environmental protection, and the fight against diseases linked to environmental degradation.

05 Nutrition

This area ties in with the objective of fighting malnutrition.

06 Food security

An area linked to health and protection within the multisectoral approach set out in the statutes.

07 Community-based rehabilitation

Physiotherapy, physical rehabilitation and support for people with reduced mobility.

08 Education

Education is one of the areas of intervention, within the multisectoral approach.

09 Disaster preparedness

Disaster preparedness and risk reduction.

10 Demining

Demining and accident prevention, as an area distinct from disaster preparedness.

11 Agro-pastoral

An area of intervention linked to the make-up of the organisation, which includes members from the agro-pastoral field.

How we work

The strategies below are those that can be drawn from the statutes and the mission. The work remains centred on the person and their dignity, with communities, in line with humanitarian principles and international humanitarian law — from prevention to rehabilitation — in conjunction with grassroots structures, partners and the competent authorities.

  • An integrated approach: care, prevention, protection, community participation and the determinants of health.
  • Priority for people with little or no access to care, and for those kept apart by conflict, displacement, disasters or exclusion.
  • Responses adapted to the context: chronic crisis, emergency, post-emergency, development, rehabilitation or construction.
  • Removing the financial, linguistic, cultural, administrative and legal barriers that block access to care.
  • Mobilising communities, involved in design and implementation.
  • Advocacy and bearing witness: raising the alarm, documenting, taking part in the relevant forums.
  • Linking sectors: health, protection, gender, inclusion, environment, nutrition, education, food security, rehabilitation, agro-pastoral.
  • Creating or organising medical, surgical and pharmaceutical facilities and activities, as needed.

The people CHEM stands with

  • Women facing inequality, sexual and gender-based violence, or unwanted pregnancies.
  • People in emergency situations, and populations affected by crises and conflict.
  • People with reduced mobility, isolated or stigmatised people.
  • Children, older people, detainees, and people who struggle to make their needs heard.
  • Populations exposed to the consequences of environmental degradation.
  • Displaced people and refugees, when access to care is closed to them.
  • Excluded or marginalised people.
  • Disaster-affected and vulnerable populations, and people living with disabling illnesses.

Where CHEM works

The head office is in Bukavu, South Kivu. The statutes open the work to the whole Democratic Republic of the Congo and, under certain conditions, beyond the country. In the initial phase, South Kivu province comes first. Branch offices may be opened where needs justify it and where a presence remains consistent with the association's objectives.

Priorities

These priorities come from the statutes. They show where CHEM is heading.

  1. Strengthen access to basic care for vulnerable and marginalised people.
  2. Build the capacity to respond to health, humanitarian, epidemic and disaster emergencies.
  3. Consolidate work in health, protection, gender, inclusion, nutrition, WASH and the environment.
  4. Strengthen community participation and empowerment.
  5. Develop advocacy, early warning, bearing witness and participation in forums.
  6. Strengthen medical, surgical, pharmaceutical and community facilities and services, as needed.
  7. Gradually extend the work beyond South Kivu, according to needs, resources and the decisions of the governing bodies.
  8. Consolidate partnerships, resource mobilisation and governance.
  9. Acquire the tools needed for quality, results monitoring, transparency and accountability.
